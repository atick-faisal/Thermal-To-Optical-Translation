Thermal-to-visible translation with a detector in the training loop

When does a thermal→visible translator that stays faithful to the visible image, trained against a frozen visible detector, let visible-trained detectors work on thermal-only imagery better than direct thermal detection or registration-based label transfer — and does it do so without inventing or erasing safety-relevant components?

thermal frametranslatedvisible frame

Where we stand: the detection loop's gain now holds on all three datasets, and it survives scoring by a detector from a different family that never took part in training. All six success criteria are now measured: four are met, and two are reported with their limits. On our power-line set the loop beats ModTr, the closest published method, by a wide margin. On the public FLIR set ModTr beats us, and the loop only reaches raw-thermal accuracy there. The last required experiment, the registration-error axis, is running on the server now. The paper is going to IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement, submitted by 31 January 2027.

Literature review

Six bodies of work meet here, and each leaves a gap

Why this matters: a detector trained on visible photos almost completely fails on thermal images of the same scene. On our validation set it scores 0.19 mAP@50 on raw thermal against 0.92 on the real photo F02. Thermal images are also hard to label by hand, and some surveys exist only in thermal. Translation needs registered pairs once, for training. After that it needs only a thermal image.

Training a translator against a frozen detector is not new: HalluciDet and ModTr do it. We claim no "first". What remains open is whether a translator that stays faithful to the visible image can carry the detector loss, whether its gain transfers to other detectors, and when translation beats the alternatives.

The method, and where each line of inquiry stands

Key findings

What the experiments have shown

Detection numbers are mAP@50 (0–1, higher is better), scored zero-shot on each dataset's validation split by an independently trained judge that never saw a translated image. Our set: 4 classes, 153 images. FLIR: car, person and bicycle, 1,013 images. MSRS: the two passive classes, car_stop and color_cone. Small grey codes (F35, rec. 024) point to the experiment ledger.

Finding 1 · consistency

The loop's gain holds on all three datasets, but on public data translation only reaches thermal's own level

On every dataset, the translator trained with the loop beats the same translator trained without it, and all six paired seeds agree each time (p = 0.031, the smallest value six seeds can give): +0.051 on ours F35, +0.351 on FLIR F136, +0.167 on MSRS F168.

The negative half: on FLIR, the loop lands on the raw-thermal floor (0.4415 against 0.4499) F137. On MSRS it stays 0.204 below a detector trained on thermal labels F169. That gap is not specific to the passive classes; the loop is below it on every class F170. We closed the passive-class claim as negative on 15 October.

What this means: the loop prevents a loss that plain translation incurs, and that holds everywhere. "Translation beats raw thermal" depends on the dataset. It does on ours, where components are nearly invisible in thermal. On FLIR's cars and people, which are already visible in thermal, it does not. goal.md treats this as a reported outcome, not a criterion.

Caveat: on FLIR the no-loop translator collapses on 7 of 24 stage checkpoints and the loop on none F139. Part of the FLIR gain is the loop keeping training stable.

Finding 2 · transfer

A detector from a different family confirms the gain

Every result above was scored by a YOLO11s judge. YOLO is the same family as the detector inside the loop, so the gain might in principle be a YOLO-specific trick. We trained a second judge from a different family, RT-DETR-l, on visible images only, and re-scored every final-stage export.

The gain holds in every campaign and keeps 85–99% of its size F182. It is significant on our GAN, FLIR and MSRS campaigns (p = 0.031). The diffusion translator has three seeds, so all three agree but cannot reach significance (p = 0.25).

What this means: Transfer, one of the six criteria, is met. The loop teaches the translator something any detector can read, not a quirk of the one it trained with.

Caveat: FLIR's RT-DETR judge is its epoch-1 checkpoint. Its training went unstable at epoch 47 and never recovered F187.

Finding 3 · margin

Against ModTr: far ahead on our data, clearly behind on FLIR

First, a null about our own instrument. The original criterion asked for +2 mAP@50 over the strongest baseline with a 95% interval excluding zero. Bootstrapping 100 of the 153 validation images, the interval's half-width reaches 9.41 points, nearly five times the bar, and even the loop against its own control is at least 3.03 F189 F190. As goal.md specified in advance, Margin became non-inferiority to ModTr, with a 5-point margin fixed before scoring.

On our set the loop clears ModTr by +13.8 (GAN) and +15.1 (diffusion) F200. On FLIR it trails by 8.6 on the 3-class primary, with the whole interval past the margin F210.

What this means: on FLIR, ModTr is the best zero-shot translator we measured F207, and its images are the furthest from the real photo (LPIPS 0.77 against our 0.42) F208. There, being detector-friendly and being visible-faithful pull apart. Our method chooses faithfulness, and the paper will report the cost on FLIR as a characterised limit.

Caveat: the ModTr we reimplemented is weaker on our set than our own no-loop translator (0.7121 against 0.8084) F201. So on our data, the stronger evidence is still loop against no-loop.

Finding 4 · annotation budget

With the loop, translation is worth about 170–215 hand-labelled thermal images

We trained thermal detectors on growing numbers of labelled thermal images and found where they catch up with translation, which uses no thermal labels at all.

On our set, translation without the loop matches ≈150 labelled images, and with the loop ≈214 F81. On MSRS's passive classes the loop matters more. Without it translation is worth only ≈48 images. With it, it is worth ≈173, turning 9% of the training split into 32% F178 F179.

What this means: this is the annotation axis of the "when does translation pay" map. Translation is worth it when thermal labels are scarce, which is the situation with thermal-only survey footage.

Conceded: with all 600 labels, a thermal-trained detector wins (0.9300 against the loop's 0.8487) F82. goal.md lists beating fully supervised thermal detection as a non-goal.

Finding 5 · faithfulness

The loop does not buy detections by inventing objects, with one failed test

The risk with any detector-in-the-loop method is that the translator paints fake components to please the detector. Before scoring, we fixed a margin for each rate in each campaign, equal to the no-loop arm's seed-to-seed spread. A test passes when the 95% interval for loop − no-loop stays inside that margin. The scoring detector is RT-DETR, which was never in the loop.

11 of 12 pass F213. On FLIR and MSRS every rate moves in the loop's favour. The failure is the diffusion translator's invented-object rate. Its average change is +0.004, well inside the margin, but with three seeds the interval reaches +0.054, past the margin of 0.032. We report it as a failure. We did not retune the margin or add seeds afterwards.

Disclosed in the paper: with no held-out split, the margins come from the same validation data they are scored on. Our GAN's invented-object pass also crosses zero (p = 0.156). That shows no difference, not proven equivalence.

Finding 6 · methodology

Lessons from these six weeks that hold regardless of the headline

  • A +2-point claim needs more images than any of our splits has.On our validation split one seed's interval reaches a half-width of 9.41 points, and the spread between seeds is as large as one seed's image noise F189 F192. A one-seed interval would understate the uncertainty, so every margin test averages over seeds.
  • A judge's best checkpoint can be its first.FLIR's RT-DETR peaked at epoch 1, drifted down, then hit numerical failure at epoch 47 and never recovered F187. We would have missed it without reading every epoch.
  • Some detectors cannot be made bit-reproducible.Same command, seed and GPU: RT-DETR gave 0.6798 against 0.6468 after 3 epochs, because one of its GPU operations has no deterministic version F188.
  • Absolute hallucination rates belong to the judge.On the same FLIR images, RT-DETR's invented-object rate is 3.3× YOLO's F185. We compare only paired loop − no-loop differences under one judge, never raw rates across judges.
  • Calibrate a baseline's budget before running it, and say when the trigger is noise.ModTr's training length was fixed by a calibration run we specified in advance: 400 epochs on ours, 339 on FLIR. FLIR's stopping rule fired inside the curve's own noise, and we say so F193 F196 F197.
  • A shared server needs memory budgeting, not only GPU budgeting.The 10 November crash was the machine's total memory commitment, not the GPUs: every data-loader worker on Windows reserves about 2 GB.
Side quest · paper 2

The enhancer cascade failed its gate, and stays out of the January paper

The idea: run a second-stage enhancer on the loop's output to restore faint components, such as fuses and switches, that the translator loses. We used PiSA-SR, a diffusion super-resolution model, as it comes, with no extra training. The risk is that an enhancer invents components. So before the first score we fixed a gate of 14 tests, to be passed by 10 January 2027.

The test: we erased 234 fuses and switches from the validation images and asked whether the cascade puts them back without making anything else worse.

What this means: a zero-shot enhancer does not restore faint components. It erases more of them. goal.md admits the cascade into this paper only if it passes its gate, so it stays out. Paper 2 continues separately.

Now

The registration-error axis

Label transfer — drawing boxes on the visible photo and moving them onto the thermal image — is only as good as the registration between the two cameras. Translation needs registered pairs only once, to train. goal.md requires us to measure how fast each route degrades as alignment worsens. We add a known misalignment of 3, 6, 12 or 24 pixels to the training pairs, and score every level on validation images that are left untouched.

Towards the paper

Readiness against the six success criteria

The rule changed on 29 October. Every criterion is measured and reported, passed or not, and drafting no longer waits for all six to hold. A criterion that fails goes into the paper as a characterised limit, with its numbers. All six are now measured: four are met, and two are reported with mixed results.

Datasets

Ours: 753 registered thermal/visible pairs from Brazil, 640×480, split 600 train / 153 validation, with 4 classes (Fuse, Pole, Switch, Transformer). There is no held-out test split, so validation is the reporting split, as on every public set. The data is never released. FLIR-aligned: a matched 600-pair training subset, 1,013 validation. MSRS daytime: 536 / 179.

How results are scored

One shared evaluation path for every method. Headline accuracy comes from judges that never train on anything this project produces: YOLO11s, and RT-DETR-l as the outside family. Significance uses exact paired sign-flip tests across seeds, never a bootstrap interval alone.

Reading the metrics

mAP@50: detection accuracy, 0–1, higher is better. A point is 0.01 mAP@50. LPIPS: perceptual distance to the real visible image, lower is better. Seed: one independent training run. p = 0.031: the strongest result six seeds can give.
