Thermal-to-visible translation with a detector in the training loop

Does feeding a detection signal back into a thermal→visible translator during training make power-line components easier to detect, without inventing or erasing safety-relevant components?

Literature review

Five bodies of work meet here, and each leaves a gap

No single line of prior work answers our question. The table shows what each area already does, what it leaves open, and which of our contributions fills the gap.

AreaWhat existsWhat is missingWe fill it with
Detection-driven infrared–visible fusionSeAFusion (Information Fusion) · DetFusion (ACM MM 2022) · TarDAL (CVPR 2022) · MetaFusion (CVPR 2023)

A detection or segmentation loss is fed back into the network that fuses a thermal and a visible image. This proves a task signal can shape image generation.

Every method needs both cameras at inference time, and all use GAN or CNN generators, not diffusion. TarDAL's released code does not actually pass the detection gradient to its generator, and MetaFusion releases no training code.

The translator is trained open-loop, with no detector feedback, and is judged by image-quality scores or by detectors fine-tuned on its own output. Those can rank methods in reverse order (our Finding 5).

Reward tuning targets aesthetics and prompt alignment in text-to-image models. It has not been applied to detection consistency in safety-critical translation, and its reward-hacking risk has not been measured there.

Experimental directions

The method, and where each line of inquiry stands

A translator turns thermal frames into visible-looking images. During training, a frozen detector scores those images and its loss flows back into the translator. A separate detector, trained independently, scores every result.

Key findings

What the experiments have shown so far

All detection numbers: mAP@50 (higher is better) on the 153-image, 423-object validation split, scored zero-shot by an independently trained YOLO11s that never saw a translated image.

Finding 1 · translation

Translation recovers most of the detection lost on thermal frames

A detector trained on visible images almost completely fails on raw thermal frames. Fuses and switches effectively disappear. After translation, the same unmodified detector reaches 84% of its real-visible accuracy, with no loop involved.

What this means: thermal frames are not information-poor for this task. The obstacle is the domain gap, and translation bridges most of it.

Finding 2 · dose

The first null result was a dosing problem, not a failed idea

Our first 12-run test of the loop found nothing. When we measured the training objective directly, the detection signal made up only 0.9–2.3% of it, about 19× smaller than the perceptual term it competed with. We recalibrated it to 10–20% and repeated the full experiment.

At the real dose, the gain grows steadily with loop strength across the three training stages. A lucky draw would not produce that pattern.

What this means: the two campaigns side by side are the dose–response argument. Both go in the paper, and the loop weight must be reported as its effective value.

Gain from the loop (loop − no loop, mAP@50), mean of 6 paired seeds
Calibrated dose (10–20% of objective) First campaign (0.9–2.3%)

Stage 0 has no loop in either arm, so its value is pure run-to-run noise. Loop strength rises across stages 1–3.

Finding 3 · causality

At the calibrated dose, the loop causes a significant detection gain

Twelve runs with identical budgets, where the only difference between paired runs is the loop. At the pre-registered endpoint (final stage), the loop adds +0.051 mAP@50. All six seeds moved the same way (exact sign-flip test, p = 0.031, the smallest value six seeds can give).

The looped runs also become much more consistent. By the final stage they vary 2.4× less across seeds than the no-loop runs.

Honest caveat: the no-loop noise check at stage 0 came out wide (−0.040). A second analysis that removes it points the same way and is larger (+0.091), but it is only suggestive (p = 0.094). Per-class claims (e.g. Switch) are not statistically supportable with 6 seeds.

Finding 4 · faithfulness

The loop does not buy detections by inventing objects

The worry with any detector-in-the-loop method is reward hacking: the translator paints fake components to please the detector. If that happened, invented objects would rise. They fell instead, and fewer real components were erased.

Image quality holds up. The loop's cost is bounded at no more than ~0.02 LPIPS and may be zero, and the perceptual terms of the training loss are unchanged.

Honest caveat: invented-object rate is the one metric independent of the detection gain, and its decrease is favourable but not significant (p = 0.156).

Finding 5 · methodology

Lessons that hold regardless of the headline result

  • Two common ways of measuring detection on translated images rank methods in opposite order.Fine-tuning a detector on each method's output saturates (> 0.9 even on raw thermal) and flips the ranking; zero-shot scoring does not. Our headline numbers are zero-shot.
  • The nominal loss weight is misleading.A weight "λ = 3" actually ran at 0.03 because of an internal scaling factor. We report the share of the objective each term really takes.
  • Noise sets the experiment size.Seed-to-seed spread is ≈ 0.053 mAP@50. Six paired seeds is the minimum for any distribution-free significance claim.
  • A published baseline has a silent bug.TarDAL's released code never backpropagates the detection loss into its generator. Our comparison fixes this, and the paper will say so.

Our dataset

853 registered thermal/visible pairs, 640×480. 600 train / 153 validation; ~100 held out as a test set that no experiment has touched. 4 annotated classes: Fuse, Pole, Switch, Transformer.

How results are scored

One shared evaluation path for every method. Headline accuracy comes from an independent detector that never trains on anything this project produces. Significance uses exact paired sign-flip tests across seeds.

Reading the metrics

mAP@50: detection accuracy, 0–1, higher is better. LPIPS: perceptual distance to the real visible image, lower is better. Seed: one independent training run.
